* Frontend authority

=scheme/grammar= is the declarative concrete-syntax authority. Its
=syntax-case= DSL expands into a =poo-flow=/=gerbil-poo= grammar object and
generates the tables consumed by =gql-syntax=. The Rust lexer emits tokens and
the parser emits Rowan events directly into one lossless Rowan tree. Every
source byte, including whitespace, comments, unknown characters, malformed
delimiters, empty input, and trailing newlines, is represented in that tree.
=gql-syntax::SyntaxNode= and the =gql-ast= lowering adapter are typed views over
that Rowan tree; they do not construct a second owned CST or reparse source
spans.

Precedence, keyword spellings, syntax shapes, and recovery codes are defined
once in that Gerbil object. The Rust parser consumes generated Pratt binding
powers. Structural expression nodes are retained in Rowan and lowered into the
graph-semantic IR. The full boundary is recorded in
=docs/architecture/0002-gerbil-grammar-authority.org=.

* Graph-semantic boundary

The ISO semantic path is graph-pattern-first. A node-only =MATCH= is valid and
does not require an edge, relation provider, or backend catalog entry. The
canonical IR has first-class graph, pattern, node, edge, binding, direction,
label, filter, =LET=, and projection concepts.

=gql-catalog= contains only ISO catalog, graph, schema, node-type, edge-type,
property, and procedure declarations. Language-neutral reasoning identities,
relations, queries, rules, lineage, transitions, and bundles live in the
corresponding =mrr-*= crates. There is no =gql-reasoning= or =gql-ascent=
compatibility surface. GQL and Cypher enter MRR only through the explicit
=mrr-frontends= lowering boundary, and evaluation is admitted through the
=meta-relational-reasoning= facade.

* ISO modular conformance configuration

ISO coverage is configured as a modular registry rather than one expanding
feature list. =standard_releases= records editions and supplements, =modules=
groups related normative surface, =feature_registry= owns stable feature
identities and evidence, and =profiles= explicitly compose the modules that a
build claims to support. A later ISO supplement adds a release entry and
affected module entries; it does not silently widen =gql-frontend-v1=.

=gql-iso-audit-v1= is the complete audited inventory and makes no conformance
claim. =gql-frontend-v1= is the intentionally narrow implementation profile.
Partial and deferred features remain visible in the registry rather than being
hidden by profile selection.

* Purity boundary

=gql-source=, =gql-syntax=, =gql-ast=, =gql-types=, =gql-catalog=, =gql-sema=,
=gql-ir=, =gql-compiler=, and =gql-core= must never depend on Ascent. Cargo
features are additive, so purity-sensitive consumers should depend on
=gql-core= directly.

=mrr-ascent= is a bounded evaluator behind MRR admission. It may affect which
closure candidates are proposed, but it cannot publish a materialized closure
or alter parsing, graph-semantic IR, or valid ISO GQL meaning. The =gql= facade
is always the pure language surface and has no reasoning-backend feature.

No Ascent DSL is accepted by the GQL parser. Rulesets are registered outside
the language and exposed through typed catalog descriptors.

The complete MRR ownership and repository topology decision is recorded in
=docs/architecture/0001-mrr-workspace-ownership.org=.

= gql-rust and Meta-Relational Reasoning Architecture

Let $"parse" : "Source" -> "CST"$ be lossless,
$"lower" : "CST" -> "AST"$, and
$"analyze" : "AST" -> "Result"("IR", "Diagnostics")$. The frontend contract requires
$"bytes"("parse"(s)) = s$ and forbids a successful IR whenever semantic diagnostics
contain an admission error.

For the reasoning layer, let $M$ be the language-neutral MRR contract graph and
$L_"GQL"$ and $L_"Cypher"$ be frontend adapters. Both lower into $M$; neither may
redefine its identity, relation, rule, lineage, or transition semantics.

The detailed bundle predicate $"BundleValid"(R, F_0, P, T)$ and its Lean proof
obligations are specified in RFC 0001.

* Assurance status

The current crates establish dependency direction and a deliberately narrow ISO
frontend profile. Full ISO/IEC 39075 conformance is not claimed. The feature
ledger is a feature-family audit: it records implemented, partial, and
not-implemented standard surface separately from quality capabilities such as
lossless recovery. Every entry has a stable identity, normative reference,
positive and negative fixtures, layer status, scope notes, and validation
evidence. Clause numbers are not fabricated without the licensed normative
text.
