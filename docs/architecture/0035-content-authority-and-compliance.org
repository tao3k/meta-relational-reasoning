#+title: RFC 0035: Content Authority, Typed Obligations, and Compliance Receipts
#+status: proposed
#+options: toc:2
#+property: header-args :eval never-export :exports code

* Decision and scope

MRR shall own the *semantic* Content Graph contract: source-bound assertions,
normative contracts, typed evidence, derivation support, revision impact, and
admission of compliance conclusions.  A Content Graph is a canonical *record of
what a named authority asserted or what a bounded verifier observed*.  It is
not a declaration that every recorded proposition is universally true.  Two
sources may disagree without either being silently overwritten.

This RFC specifies a *next* semantic contract on top of this revision's
implemented Context slice.  It already has =mrr-agentic-ai-context=, facade admission,
source-bound selection and manifest restore, declared dependency closure,
revision/semantic reuse, byte materialization, token-prefix eligibility, and
Lean models and source-refinement slices.  Its =AgenticAiContextContract=
currently binds actor, task, opaque =policy_digest=, required fact IDs,
temporal receipt IDs, and a completeness flag.  Its =evidence.rs= checks fact
validity and completeness.  Neither is yet a typed obligation/evidence
verifier, =ContentGraph= authority model, or =ComplianceReceipt=.  The exact
implemented boundary remains documented in
[[file:agentic-ai-context.org][Agentic AI Context System]].  RFC 0025 owns
the system-level POO Flow boundary; RFC 0036 specifies the extension of
Context projection that consumes this RFC.  RFC 0023 remains the authority
for public truth and incompleteness states.

The name =Content Graph= denotes semantic authority and provenance, not a
required physical graph database or a new storage format.  =mrr-data= may
persist its encoded artifacts, but CID, storage availability, and conditional
head commit do not confer MRR admission.  MRR has no reverse dependency on
=mrr-data= (RFC 0026).

* Authority is typed and scoped

An admitted record has a stable typed identity, source owner, exact source
revision or semantic generation, assertion kind, scope, provenance, and
admission receipt.  Admission establishes *eligible use within that scope*;
it does not expand a test run into software correctness, a checked theorem
into deployed safety, or an author's policy into a universal rule.

| Kind | What may be admitted | What admission does not establish |
|-
| Source assertion | exact bytes and source-owner claim at a revision | independent factual truth |
| Execution observation | command, subject, environment, exit, and output evidence | correctness outside the exercised oracle and scope |
| Formal observation | exact theorem, definitions, assumptions, toolchain, and checker result | properties absent from the theorem statement |
| Reference observation | a source location and digest resolved by an owning parser/provider | that referenced code was executed or reviewed |
| Normative contract | an authorized rule for a subject, action, and time scope | empirical occurrence or automatic enforceability |
| Derived conclusion | bounded support and MRR lineage/admission at one generation | a stronger proposition than its supports justify |

An execution or proof receipt is an observation by a specified producer.  Its
trust level depends on the producer identity, verifier profile, artifact
integrity, and subject binding.  A model's account of an action, a tool's
unverified stdout, a search hit, or a physical query row is only a proposal
until the appropriate owner verifies it.  The admission result records
provenance and conflicts rather than erasing them.

The initial semantic node roles are =Source=, =Evidence=, =Contract=,
=Derivation=, and =Receipt=.  This is an API vocabulary, not a request to add
five new physical node tables.  Existing =Fact= and =RelationContext= remain
the typed relational substrate; role-specific validation prevents one generic
=Fact= from impersonating an evidence or policy receipt.  Edges such as
=defines=, =supports=, =refutes=, =requires=, =verifies=, =supersedes=, and
=depends_on= must name typed endpoints and carry a generation-bound MRR
lineage receipt where they assert a derivation.  Unknown roles or unsupported
cross-role edges fail admission.

* Contract and obligation model

A =Contract= binds the authoring source, authorized issuer, source revision,
subject selector, action selector, validity interval or external cut, rule
kind, and canonical obligation set.  The four rule kinds have distinct
semantics:

| Kind | Composition | Transition consequence |
|-
| Mandatory | inherited union; no silent deletion | specified action requires a valid witness for each applicable obligation |
| Prohibition | inherited union; deny dominates | action requires a positive, scoped guard decision; absence of a violation report is insufficient |
| Overrideable | most specific applicable authorized rule in a supplied linearization | may change only the declared override slot |
| Advisory | deterministic ordered accumulation | informs Context; never manufactures a blocking verdict |

=Obligation= names the exact predicate to establish, subject and action scope,
acceptable evidence classes, verifier profile, coverage requirement, freshness
rule, blocking behavior, and the contract revision that introduced it.
Predicates are typed; a prose instruction without a registered lowering and
verifier remains =judgment-required= or advisory, never silently becomes a
machine-checkable gate.  An unknown verifier, missing subject binding, or
ambiguous source authority is an admission error.

POO Flow owns policy selection, composition, authorization, and lifecycle.
=lean-poo= owns pure C4 mathematics; the current Context crate validates
externally supplied composition evidence and does not certify C4.  MRR may
validate that supplied ordered composition receipt and compute the
effective *semantic* obligation set; it does not implement a second C4
algorithm or decide which principal is allowed to waive a rule.  For the same
subject, action, temporal cut, and authorized source revisions, adding a child
scope preserves inherited Mandatory and Prohibition obligations.  Removing or
relaxing one requires an explicit, source-owned, authorized revision or waiver
with its own identity and scope.  A waiver is not a model-generated yes/no.
Conflicting hard rules or an unsatisfiable obligation set produce =CONFLICT=;
the system must not choose a winner by ordering alone.

#+begin_src text
effective_hard(parent, child)
  = inherited_hard(parent) union local_hard(child)
    minus explicitly_authorized_and_applicable_revisions
#+end_src

The inclusion claim is conditional on a fixed source cut and valid authority
receipts.  A source revision or authorization change creates a new evaluation;
it is not a counterexample to monotonic composition within the old cut.

* Authoring sources and admission

=AGENTS.md=, Org documents, repository policy, and a direct user declaration
are possible *authoring sources*.  Their presence in a prompt, file, search
result, or Context projection does not grant normative authority.  An adapter
must bind exact source bytes/revision, parser or structural receipt, issuer,
scope, and the requested typed obligation.  The authority owner decides
whether that issuer may define or revise that rule for the subject/action;
MRR admits the resulting semantic record.  A retrieved document cannot
silently declare a higher-priority policy for its reader.

Orgize may establish the structure of an Org source; the existing bounded
=mrr-intent= projection in RFC 0017 is a different vocabulary and cannot be
reused as a generic contract compiler without a new grammar and negative
tests.  Free-form =AGENTS.md= prose requires an explicit reviewed mapping to
typed obligations.  Model extraction can propose such a mapping, but only an
authorized owner or registered deterministic compiler may admit it.  The
same source may contain advisory prose and blocking rules; a parser must not
promote one class into the other because of formatting alone.

Changing the source revision, parser profile, issuer authority, or lowering
version invalidates the dependent effective-contract receipt.  Policy
precedence between direct user, repository, and organizational sources is
supplied by the control-plane authority model; MRR does not invent a global
priority order from document names.

* Evidence, claims, and verdicts

A model may submit a =ComplianceClaim= containing contract, obligation,
subject/action, and evidence references.  The claim is a request for checking.
Its natural-language answer or confidence is never evidence of satisfaction.
Questions may help an Agent plan but have no verification authority.

An =EvidenceReceipt= identifies its producer and verifier boundary, evidence
class, exact input and output identities or digests, source/semantic revision,
coverage, validity window, and provenance.  Profiles specialize this common
envelope:

- =ExecutionEvidence=: structured command identity and arguments, workspace
  snapshot (including relevant dirty state), toolchain and environment
  fingerprint, bounded start/end, exit status, stdout/stderr artifact digests,
  and declared tested scope.  Exit 0 alone never proves a broader requirement.
- =ProofEvidence=: statement and dependency digests, theorem/axiom inventory,
  checker and version, checked artifact digest, and the exact proposition
  validated.  A Lean process exit is not by itself a kernel soundness claim.
- =ReferenceEvidence=: provider, source revision, symbol or syntax identity,
  span, and content digest.  A valid reference proves location and identity;
  it does not prove execution or semantic review.
- =HumanJudgmentEvidence=: authenticated reviewer, authority scope, exact
  subject, decision, and reason.  It is explicitly human-attested, never
  labelled machine-verified, and is allowed only by the obligation's profile.

The initial verdict domain is =satisfied=, =refuted=, =unknown=,
=incomplete=, =stale=, =conflict=, and =judgment-required=.  This is a
compliance-specific refinement of RFC 0023's public states, not a replacement
for them.  At the public facade, =satisfied/refuted/unknown/incomplete/stale/
conflict= project respectively to =TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN/INCOMPLETE/STALE/
CONFLICT=; =judgment-required= projects to =UNKNOWN= with its reason retained.
=refuted= requires complete counterevidence for the exact
predicate; missing evidence is =unknown=.  Budget exhaustion is =incomplete=.
Old subject, contract, verifier, authority, or source revisions are =stale=.
Disagreeing admitted authorities are =conflict=.  Free-text criteria without
an accepted verifier are =judgment-required=.  Only =satisfied= can close a
blocking positive obligation, and only a positive =allow= from the applicable
guard can pass a prohibition gate.

A =ComplianceReceipt= is immutable and binds the contract and effective-set
digests, obligation ID/revision, subject and action IDs/revisions, semantic
generation, evidence IDs/digests, verifier ID/version/profile, coverage,
verdict, support lineage, and receipt digest.  A new source or evidence
revision creates a new receipt; it never edits an old one.  Receipt identity
must use canonical field ordering and domain-separated hashing.  This RFC
does not prescribe a byte encoding before the identity profile is reviewed.

* Transition enforcement and race boundary

MRR computes an eligibility receipt for a *proposed* transition at an exact
snapshot.  The POO Flow policy/authorization owner supplies its decision; the
MRR production runtime or another explicitly designated effect owner must
enforce the combined decision at the actual completion, publication, or effect
boundary.  A prompt instruction or MRR
receipt alone cannot intercept =git commit=, a remote API, or a subprocess.
The enforcing owner must compare the subject, contract set, authorization,
semantic generation, and relevant evidence dependencies immediately before
publication.  Drift denies the transition and requests reevaluation.  A
storage conditional head is an additional physical guard, not a substitute
for this semantic comparison.

Where a commit changes the subject revision, a pre-commit test receipt applies
to the tested tree digest, not automatically to the new commit identity.  The
policy must state whether the immutable tree is the subject, whether a
post-commit receipt is required, or whether a checked equivalence binds the
commit to that tree.  Reusing a receipt across changed source bytes is
forbidden.  Prohibitions that concern external effects need an owner-controlled
preflight or execution guard; a finite history with no observed violation
cannot establish that a future effect is safe.

* Invalidation and reuse

Every receipt records the *complete dependency set needed by its verifier*:
subject bytes, applicable contract set, authority and capability, verifier
profile, evidence artifacts, and relevant external revisions.  Reverse impact
through existing MRR lineage/revision contracts identifies candidates for
invalidation.  A receipt may be reused only after all recorded dependencies
and its scope still match; impact analysis alone does not certify reuse.
Revocation is an explicit dependency, even if the content bytes are identical.
When coverage is incomplete, the conservative result is =unknown= or =stale=.
[[file:0037-meta-relational-impact.org][RFC 0037]] proposes a typed meta-relational
frontier and post-change reevaluation receipt for this boundary.  Its
alternative-support and certified-absence rules prevent a reverse path from
being misread as automatic refutation, or a zero-row query as a permission.
The compliance verifier and effect owner still decide applicability and
enforce the current action cut.

* Proof and implementation gates

Formalization belongs under the existing =proofs/MRRProof/AgenticAIContext=
project, reusing its pinned =lean-poo= basis (RFC 0001 and the Agentic AI
Context System document).  The present closure/revision/restore theorems do
not imply any of the following new authority or compliance theorems.
The model must define authority and scope predicates before attempting:

1. Content admission non-escalation: a Context proposal cannot derive an
   authoritative Content record without a source-owned admission step.
2. Hard-rule preservation: for a fixed cut and no authorized revocation,
   child composition cannot remove a parent's applicable hard obligation.
3. Compliance soundness *relative to verifier assumptions*: a satisfied
   blocking obligation has an applicable, fresh, complete witness checked by
   its declared verifier.  This is not a theorem that the external world is
   safe.
4. Invalidation closure: changing any declared dependency makes the old
   receipt ineligible; unaffected dependencies may permit a new reuse receipt.
5. Transition preservation: an enforced action cannot pass with missing,
   stale, incomplete, conflicting, or merely model-asserted blocking evidence.

Counterexamples must include a child attempting to delete a parent rule, two
conflicting source owners, a green test on a different tree, a proof with an
unstated axiom, an unauthenticated human waiver, a revoked capability, and a
subject revision changing between check and commit.  A Lean proof over a model
is a design check; integration with actual guards and producers needs separate
runtime evidence.

The implementation sequence is contract/identity design, evidence profiles,
verifier admission, compliance receipts, revision impact, then transition
integration.  No new crate name is fixed here: start within existing semantic
owners and split a crate only when dependency and public API pressure justify
it.  Acceptance requires typed negative tests and an end-to-end denied
transition through the real enforcing owner.  Until then this RFC remains
proposed and no completion enforcement is claimed.

The adversarial cases and measurement plan are recorded in
[[file:../../research/content-context-compliance-agenda.org][Content, Context, and Compliance: Research Agenda]].
